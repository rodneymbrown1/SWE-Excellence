# Trace, run-time statistics, interrupts in the analysis {.chapter data-part="PART B · RTOS LABS" data-label="Lab R8"}

<p class="lede"><b>Goal.</b> See a system with a context-switch trace, read the kernel's CPU-share statistics, and show that leaving an interrupt out of the analysis understates the response. <b>Book.</b> Chapters 4 and 20. <b>File.</b> <code>labs/rtos/r8_trace/main.c</code>.</p>

The task set of Lab R2 (C/T = 1/4, 2/6, 3/12 ms) plus a timer interrupt every 2 ms costing 250 µs.

**Predict first.** (a) Worst response of t3 *without* the interrupt in the analysis? *With* it (as a top-priority task `isr:250:2000`)? (b) Per-task CPU share from C/T alone, and what the interrupt adds? (c) Which of your two predictions will the measurement match?

```
python3 labs/host/rtcalc.py t1:1000:4000 t2:2000:6000 t3:3000:12000
python3 labs/host/rtcalc.py isr:250:2000 t1:1000:4000 t2:2000:6000 t3:3000:12000
make -C labs/rtos/r8_trace && python3 labs/rtos/run.py labs/rtos/r8_trace/build/r8_trace.elf
```


**Questions.** (1) By what percentage does the analysis without the interrupt understate t3? (2) Why do the run-time statistics show t1 at 31.5% and not 25%? (3) How many context switches in the window, and how many are caused by the interrupt? (4) The trace buffer holds 1,024 events. At this rate, how many milliseconds of history is that?
